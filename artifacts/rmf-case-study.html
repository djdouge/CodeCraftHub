NIST Risk Management Framework Case Study

A simulated RMF lifecycle for the fictional HarborPoint Client Services Platform (HCSP), showing how security decisions move from business context and system categorization through control implementation, assessment, authorization, and continuous monitoring.

NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 27 RMF StepsModerate Practice AssumptionPOA&M + Monitoring
System: HCSP is a fictional environment combining managed Windows endpoints, SaaS CRM, cloud document storage, identity services, remote users, and customer PII. For this practice case, I treat the system as a Moderate-impact environment to demonstrate the RMF process. A real categorization would require authoritative information-type and impact analysis.

RMF Lifecycle

Prepare through Monitor

1

Prepare

Identify stakeholders, system owner, security responsibilities, business mission, data handled, dependencies, threat assumptions, risk tolerance, and the initial authorization boundary.

2

Categorize

Document information types and expected adverse impact to confidentiality, integrity, and availability. The practice case uses an overall Moderate assumption because the platform handles customer PII and supports important business operations.

3

Select

Start from an appropriate control baseline concept, then tailor based on technology, risk, shared services, compensating controls, and organizational requirements. Priority families include AC, IA, AU, CM, RA, IR, CP, and SI.

4

Implement

Document how each control is implemented, who owns it, which components provide it, dependencies, parameters, and what evidence should demonstrate operation.

5

Assess

Use examination, interviews, and testing to determine whether controls are implemented correctly, operating as intended, and producing the desired security outcome. Record deficiencies and supporting evidence.

6

Authorize

Present the risk picture to the authorizing decision-maker: control assessment results, open findings, POA&M, risk register, residual risk, dependencies, and conditions for approval. Leadership accepts, rejects, or conditionally accepts the risk.

7

Monitor

Track control changes, vulnerability aging, privileged access, logging coverage, incidents, recovery tests, vendor risk, configuration changes, and other triggers that could change the accepted risk posture.

Sample Authorization Package

What I would hand to a decision-maker

System Security Plan

Boundary, architecture, roles, environment, control implementation descriptions, dependencies, and responsible owners.

Security Assessment Report

Assessment scope, methods, evidence, control results, findings, severity, and assessor conclusions.

Risk Register

Prioritized threats and vulnerabilities connected to business impact, treatment decisions, owners, and residual risk.

Plan of Action & Milestones

Open weaknesses translated into accountable remediation items with milestones, due dates, resources, and verification criteria.

Metrics that keep authorization meaningful

Core RMF principle: authorization is not the finish line. Changes in technology, threats, vulnerabilities, business processes, or control effectiveness can change the risk decision, so monitoring must feed back into the lifecycle.
